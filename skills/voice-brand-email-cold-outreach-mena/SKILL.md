# MENA Cold Outreach Email

## When this applies

Use this skill whenever drafting a cold or warm-introductory email to a business contact in the MENA region — specifically UAE (onshore, DIFC, ADGM), KSA, Lebanon, Egypt, Qatar, and diaspora markets. The norms here differ meaningfully from North American or European cold email: MENA business culture is relationship-first, title-conscious, and highly sensitive to generic or templated communication.

## Structure

A well-crafted MENA cold email has exactly these components:

### 1. Subject line
- Specific and benefit-focused. Never "Quick question" or "Checking in."
- Reference the recipient's context directly where possible.
- Good: "Contract review automation for [Firm Name]'s M&A team"
- Bad: "Exciting legal AI opportunity"
- Length: 6–10 words. Mobile-first (50 characters or fewer shows cleanly in preview).

### 2. Opening line
- Personal and specific. Reference a real, recent action: a talk they gave, an article they published, a deal they announced, a case they were involved in.
- Do not use generic openers like "I hope this email finds you well" or "I came across your profile."
- Good: "I read your piece in The National on AI adoption at UAE law firms — your point about institutional knowledge transfer resonated."
- Bad: "I was browsing LinkedIn and noticed your impressive background."

### 3. Body (3–4 sentences maximum for a cold email)
Cover exactly:
- What the sender does (one sentence, specific).
- Why it is relevant to this recipient right now (one sentence — tie to their role, firm type, or a recent event in their market).
- A concrete outcome or proof point (one sentence — a firm reduced review time, a GC team cut outside counsel costs, a specific outcome, not a vague claim).

Do not include feature lists. Do not write a paragraph for each of these — three to four tight sentences is the entire body.

### 4. Call to action
- Specific and low-friction.
- Good: "Would 15 minutes next week work to walk through a live demo?"
- Bad: "Let me know if you're interested in learning more."
- Offer a specific time window or a Calendly-style link.

### 5. Sign-off
- Respectful and professional, but not stiff.
- Include: name, title, company, LinkedIn URL (one click to verify credibility).
- In Arabic correspondence: use appropriate honorifics based on the recipient's title and gender.

## Tone rules

| Do | Avoid |
|----|-------|
| Direct but warm | Aggressive or pushy |
| Confident and specific | Vague superlatives |
| Respectful of the recipient's time | Long paragraphs |
| Acknowledge regional context | US-centric assumptions |
| Professional without formality overload | Robotic corporate-speak |

MENA business contacts — especially in UAE and KSA — value relationships and trust over transactional efficiency. The email's job is to earn a conversation, not to close a deal.

## Bilingual considerations

- **Default to English** for most MENA B2B outreach at the decision-maker level; English is the working language of business in UAE, DIFC, ADGM, and much of KSA corporate law.
- **Send Arabic version** if:
  - The recipient's primary working language is known to be Arabic (e.g., government bodies, Saudi family office contacts, Lebanese lawyers in private practice).
  - The email is specifically about Arabic-language legal services.
- When sending Arabic, use Modern Standard Arabic (MSA), not dialect, for formal business communication. Gulf dialect is acceptable only for follow-ups to established contacts.
- Do not send a bilingual single email (Arabic + English in one message) — it reads as uncertainty. Choose one language per send; offer the other if they reply in it.

## Follow-up cadence

| Sequence | Timing | Content |
|----------|--------|---------|
| Email 1 | Day 0 | Full cold email as described above |
| Email 2 | Day 7 (if no response) | Short follow-up — one paragraph, reference the first email, add one new piece of value or context |
| Email 3 | Day 14 (if no response) | Final "breakup" email — gracious, leaves the door open, offers a resource link |
| Stop | After 3 attempts | Do not contact again without a new trigger or referral |

The follow-up emails are progressively shorter, not longer. Do not resend the full pitch in email 2.

## What not to do

- Do not use fake urgency ("respond by Friday or the offer expires").
- Do not name-drop unless the mutual connection has explicitly agreed to be used as a reference.
- Do not send identical emails to multiple people at the same firm — if they compare notes, it destroys credibility.
- Do not attach a deck or brochure to the first cold email — it increases spam filter scoring and signals that you are mass-mailing.
