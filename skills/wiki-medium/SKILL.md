# Medium and Substack for Legal-Tech Content

## Scope

This pack covers the strategic use of Medium and Substack as secondary content distribution channels for a legal-tech product. Both platforms can expand reach beyond the product's own blog, but they serve different purposes and audiences. This pack helps decide when to use each and how to optimise for each platform.

---

## Platform comparison

| Dimension | Medium | Substack |
|---|---|---|
| Primary use case | Article distribution; discoverability via Medium algorithm | Newsletter + blog; direct subscriber relationship |
| Discovery mechanism | Medium curators, tags, Partner Program | Word-of-mouth; cross-promotion; Substack Discover |
| SEO | Limited (Medium pages rank in Google; canonical issues if cross-posting) | Limited (Substack pages indexed but rarely outrank owned domain) |
| Monetisation | Medium Partner Program (payment per read-time) | Paid subscriptions to newsletter |
| Audience fit | Tech/startup/VC community; some legal-adjacent readers | Direct subscribers; best for relationship-building with a defined audience |
| Arabic content | Poor — platform is English-primary; no RTL support | Poor — similar limitations |
| Brand control | Low — Medium branding dominates | Medium — Substack allows some customisation but is clearly a Substack publication |

---

## When to use Medium

Medium is appropriate for:
- **Thought leadership amplification**: a well-crafted analysis of a legal-tech trend or MENA regulatory development may reach the Medium tech/startup community (investors, founders, journalists) better than posting only on the product blog
- **Cross-posting with canonical tag**: publishing a canonical version on the product blog and cross-posting to Medium (with Medium's canonical URL feature) can extend reach without SEO penalty
- **Access to the Partner Program audience**: if the article is submitted to Medium publications (e.g. Towards Data Science for technical articles, or legal/startup publications on Medium), it can reach established audiences

Avoid Medium for:
- Primary legal-professional audience (legal practitioners generally do not read Medium)
- Arabic-language content (no RTL support)
- Articles that must rank in Google search for legal queries (use the owned blog with proper SEO)

---

## When to use Substack

Substack is appropriate for:
- **Newsletter as product**: if the strategy is to build a directly subscribed audience of legal professionals who trust the brand and want regular updates, Substack provides an easy setup and distribution infrastructure
- **Audience before product**: pre-launch, a Substack newsletter about MENA legal developments can build a subscriber list that converts to product users at launch
- **Long-form thought leadership with a personal voice**: Substack works best when there is a named author with a consistent perspective; it suits a founder's voice better than a brand voice

Avoid Substack for:
- Replacing the owned newsletter (if the email marketing tool already captures subscribers, fragmenting between Substack and the owned list creates confusion)
- SEO-primary content (Substack articles rarely outrank owned domain in search)
- Arabic-language newsletters (RTL and Arabic typography support is poor)

## Cross-posting strategy

### Canonical approach (recommended)

1. Publish the primary version on the owned blog at `yoursite.com/blog/[article-slug]`
2. Wait 3–7 days for Google to index the owned version
3. Cross-post to Medium using the "Import story" feature, which automatically sets the canonical URL to the original post
4. Optionally cross-post to LinkedIn Articles (separate from LinkedIn posts)

This approach maximises SEO benefit on the owned domain while expanding distribution.

### Exclusive Substack approach

If the goal is to build a dedicated newsletter audience rather than drive traffic to the product blog, publish content exclusively on Substack. Do not cross-post to the owned blog; the Substack subscriber relationship is the asset.

## Content types that perform on each platform

### Medium

- "How I used [technology] to solve [problem]" — technical narrative
- "The state of legal-tech in [year]" — market analysis
- "[Company] built X in Y days" — founder story / build-in-public
- "Why [conventional wisdom] is wrong about legal AI" — contrarian take

### Substack

- Weekly digest: top 3 legal developments in MENA + AI impact
- Deep-dive analysis: one topic per issue, 800–1,500 words
- "Ask the AI" column: reader questions answered using the product
- Behind-the-scenes: product development updates, what the team is learning

## Audience building tactics

For Substack:
- Include a Substack link in every email signature from the team
- Cross-promote with adjacent Substack newsletters (legal, tech, MENA startup)
- Offer a valuable free download (template, checklist) in exchange for subscription
- Announce each new issue on LinkedIn with a "link in bio" to the Substack

For Medium:
- Submit to relevant Medium publications for curator distribution
- Tag articles correctly (Legal, Law, LegalTech, Startup, Middle East)
- Engage with comments quickly — Medium's algorithm rewards engagement in the first 24 hours

---

## Caveats & currency

Both Medium and Substack have changed their algorithms, monetisation models, and discoverability features multiple times. The dynamics above reflect early 2026 platform behaviour. Check current platform documentation before making significant content investments. The owned blog and email list should always be the primary channel; Medium and Substack are amplification layers.
