# Leadership and People Management

## Scope

This pack covers the management practices that produce high-performing, healthy teams at a legal-tech startup: 1:1 structures, performance frameworks, calibration, compensation philosophy, and growth planning. It is oriented toward founders and managers building small-to-medium MENA-context teams.

---

## 1:1s

### Purpose

The 1:1 is the single highest-leverage management tool. Its purpose is not status reporting (that belongs in standups and async updates) — it is building trust, unblocking the individual, and creating a feedback channel that cannot exist in group settings.

### Recommended structure

A 30-minute weekly 1:1:

| Time | Content |
|---|---|
| 0–5 min | Temperature check — what's on the person's mind? |
| 5–15 min | Current work: blockers, context the manager can provide, decisions to make |
| 15–25 min | Growth: recent wins, development areas, longer-term ambitions |
| 25–30 min | Manager's input: feedback, context from above, anything the person needs to know |

The **person's agenda comes first**, not the manager's. If the manager dominates every 1:1, the meeting becomes a status check-in and loses its trust-building function.

### 1:1 anti-patterns

- Skipping 1:1s when things are busy (this is when they matter most)
- Turning them into project status updates
- Always letting the manager talk more than the report
- No documentation of follow-up items (leads to the same issues reappearing every week)

## Performance frameworks

### Levels and expectations

Define clear expectations per level for each role before the first performance conversation. For an engineering team:

| Level | Core expectation |
|---|---|
| L3 (junior) | Completes well-defined tasks independently; asks for help appropriately |
| L4 (mid) | Owns features end-to-end; identifies edge cases; reviews junior work |
| L5 (senior) | Designs systems; mentors; identifies problems before they're assigned |
| L6 (staff/principal) | Drives technical direction across teams; org-level impact |

For legal professionals on the team (legal PMs, legal specialists), adapt the levels to reflect legal domain expertise alongside product/technical contributions.

### Performance reviews

Cadence: formal reviews twice per year; lightweight check-ins quarterly. Structure:

1. **Self-assessment** — what did you accomplish? where did you fall short? what do you want to work on?
2. **Manager assessment** — calibrated against the level expectations; specific examples required
3. **Calibration** — managers meet to compare assessments across the team; prevents grade inflation and ensures consistency
4. **Feedback conversation** — the output of the review is a clear, direct conversation; no surprises (feedback should not first appear in the written review)

### Performance improvement plans (PIPs)

A PIP should never be a surprise — it follows a documented pattern of missed expectations and direct feedback. In MENA employment law contexts (particularly UAE Labour Law and KSA Labour Law), formal disciplinary procedures may affect entitlements on termination; get employment law advice before starting a formal PIP process.

## Compensation philosophy

A transparent, principled compensation philosophy reduces negotiation friction and perceived unfairness. Key decisions:

### Market positioning

Where does the company pay relative to the market? Options:
- **50th percentile (market)** — sustainable; compensates with equity and mission
- **75th percentile** — competitive; attracts senior talent; requires strong funding
- **25th percentile + equity-heavy** — risky; works only in the earliest stage with strong mission alignment

For a legal-AI startup targeting scarce bilingual legal-tech talent in MENA, targeting the 60th–70th percentile for engineers and legal specialists is the right default. Underpricing causes churn; overpricing burns runway.

### Compensation components

| Component | Notes |
|---|---|
| Base salary | Primary comparison benchmark; review annually |
| Housing allowance | Standard in UAE/KSA employment; often structured as a separate line item |
| Health insurance | Mandatory in UAE (all emirates) and KSA for employees |
| Annual bonus | Discretionary; tie to company and individual performance |
| Equity (ESOP) | 4-year vest, 1-year cliff; refresh grants for retention at senior levels |
| End-of-service gratuity | Mandatory under UAE Federal Labour Law; 21 days per year of service for first 5 years |

### Calibration

Compensation calibration sessions (separate from performance calibration) compare salaries across the team to check for internal equity drift. Common issues: new hires at higher salaries than long-tenured employees at the same level; gender pay gaps; role-title inflation. Catch these annually before they become retention or morale problems.

## Growth plans

### Individual development plans (IDPs)

Every person on the team should have a clear answer to "what do I need to demonstrate to reach the next level?" An IDP is a 6–12 month plan that makes this concrete:

- 2–3 specific skills or behaviours to develop
- Projects or assignments that will build those skills
- Check-in cadence (typically monthly with the manager)
- Success metrics (how will we both know it's working?)

### Growth paths

Not everyone wants to manage. Define dual tracks:
- **Management track**: team lead → manager → director → VP
- **Individual contributor track**: senior → staff → principal → distinguished engineer

Both tracks should be respected and compensated comparably at equivalent levels. Legal-AI specifically benefits from deep IC specialists (a principal legal-AI engineer who understands both the law and the model behaviour is rare and valuable; promoting them into management wastes that expertise).

## MENA team dynamics

### Communication style

MENA professional culture tends toward indirect communication of criticism (particularly in group settings) and high-context communication (what is not said matters as much as what is said). Managers from low-context cultural backgrounds should:
- Not interpret the absence of objection as agreement
- Create explicit private channels for concerns (the 1:1 is the right place)
- Deliver critical feedback privately before any group discussion

### Hierarchy

MENA workplace culture often has stronger hierarchical norms than North American or Northern European contexts. Team members may be reluctant to challenge a manager's decision even when they have better information. Actively create psychological safety:
- Explicitly invite challenge ("I want to know if you think I'm wrong")
- Acknowledge mistakes publicly and quickly
- Credit team members' ideas explicitly

### Religious practice

In a team that includes observant Muslims, be aware of prayer times, Ramadan fasting (which affects energy levels and work patterns), and the importance of the Friday prayer and weekend (Friday–Saturday in most GCC countries, Friday only in Lebanon). Schedule key meetings and deadlines with these in mind.

## Caveats & currency

Employment law in UAE and KSA changes with amendments to the Labour Law and associated ministerial decrees. The UAE Federal Decree-Law No. 33/2021 on Labour Relations significantly updated the UAE employment framework. Specific termination procedures, notice periods, and gratuity calculations must be verified against current law and (for DIFC employees) DIFC Employment Law separately. Get employment legal advice before any termination or PIP in a MENA context.
